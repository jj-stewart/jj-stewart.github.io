As a Ph.D. candidate at Maynooth University, my research focuses on the accretion and ejection process of Young Stellar Objects (YSOs), in particular Herbig Ae/Be objects and Classical T Tauri Stars (CTTS). I find these YSOs interesting as they are a testbed for understanding star and planet formation.

I am a member of Dr. Emma Whelan's Star and Planet formation group. I work on the CARINA project run by Dr. Whelan, which uses machine learning tools on large astronomical datasets such as Integral Field Spectroscopy (IFS) and Time-Domain surveys (TDS). The TDS is done using the Mauve Space Telescope for which I am a member of the Science team, while the IFS uses instruments such as the Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE) or Enhanced Resolution Imager and Spectrograph (ERIS) on the European Southern Observatory's (ESO) Very Large Telescope (VLT).

My academic journey began with a Bachelor of Physics with Astrophysics degree from Maynooth University, where I graduated with a First Class Honours. My undergraduate thesis entitled "Variabilty of the Jet Driving Classical T Tauri Star DG Tau" was an optical and near-infrared study of the physical properties of DG Tau's Jet using the 1.2m telescope in L'Observatoire de Haute Provence and ERIS on the VLT.
